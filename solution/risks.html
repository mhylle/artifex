Chapter 6 · Risks & Safeguards

Designed against the documented
ways this goes wrong

Systems like SWARM have been tried in parts, and the parts have been measured failing in specific, repeatable ways. This chapter is the premortem made structural: each documented failure mode, and the mechanism in this design that exists specifically to answer it.

Where multi-agent systems actually fail

Premortem — six ways it died, prevented

"The metrics were green and the output was garbage."

The Learning Agent optimized pass rates; reviewers drifted lenient; every dashboard improved while customers got worse results.

Warning signs

Pass rates rising while human spot-check satisfaction falls; verdicts getting shorter; red-flag rate dropping to zero.

Prevention

Sealed bench the learner can't see; calibration probes that must get caught; periodic human audit sampled from the dial; constitution keeps "pass" meaning pass.

"It produced a beautiful org chart and no value."

Decomposition became the product: exquisite trees, thousands of contracts, endless staffing — and mission outcomes no better than one good agent with a long afternoon.

Warning signs

Meta-work share of budget climbing; tiny missions spawning huge trees; value-per-effort flat or falling versus the single-agent baseline.

Prevention

The delegate-whole gate; budget ceilings on meta-work itself; the swarm's fitness function is value-per-effort against baselines, not task counts.

"One confident hallucination spread everywhere."

A worker wrote a wrong "fact" into the commons; forty later missions retrieved it; by discovery, the contamination had provenance-washed itself through derived findings.

Warning signs

Commons entries cited far beyond their evidence weight; findings whose provenance chains all lead back to one task.

Prevention

Quarantine plus independent re-derivation for high-impact entries; expiry forces re-earning; provenance chains stay queryable so contamination is traceable and revocable in one sweep.

"It never finished, and never admitted it."

A mission with contradictory criteria cycled the escalation ladder for days, each loop plausible in isolation, burning budget in dignified circles.

Warning signs

Same task climbing the ladder twice; escalation spend outpacing execution spend; plan rewrites without plan changes.

Prevention

Stopping conditions and stall counters are contract fields, not habits; ladder-exhaustion and budget thresholds force the surrender decision; contradictory criteria are Gate A's job to catch at intake time.

"We drowned in our own audit trail."

Everything was logged; nothing could be found. The Learning Agent read raw traces like tea leaves — automated root-cause localization on unstructured traces measures ~11% accuracy.

Warning signs

Learning cycles producing vague hypotheses; investigations taking longer than the work investigated.

Prevention

Structure at write time, not read time: typed events, criterion ids, error classes, contract keys. The ledger is designed around the queries learning will ask — localization as lookup, not archaeology.

"The library became a museum of lucky auditions."

Designs promoted on single good runs; champions entrenched; the Agent Creator kept picking the same aging stars for work that had drifted away from them.

Warning signs

Library reuse rate high but per-category compliance falling; promotion decisions untraceable to replicated evidence; one design dominating a category.

Prevention

Clade scores over auditions; standing re-earned continuously; Pareto diversity per category; down-weighting keeps selection honest without destroying stepping stones.

Forbidden by design

Anti-patterns the architecture rules out structurally — not by asking agents nicely:

  • No free agent-to-agent chatter. Coordination without a record is how systems become undebuggable. All context moves through the broker; all work moves through contracts.
  • No task without a contract; no contract without stopping conditions. Unbounded tasks are the documented top failure class, pre-answered at the schema level.
  • No worker sees its own scorer. Verification plans stay out of executing agents' contexts — visibility is the measured 43× difference between honest work and gamed work.
  • No judge inside the learner's write scope. The one lesson every self-improving system has taught, made constitutional.
  • No blind retries. Every rejection carries an error class; the ladder rung is chosen by diagnosis, never by hope.
  • No wholesale rewrites of accumulated assets. Playbooks and prompts change by itemized deltas — monolithic rewriting measurably erodes the detail that made assets valuable.
  • No silent capping or dropping. Whatever the swarm chose not to do — tasks skipped, coverage bounded, retries abandoned — is stated in the deliverable, because omissions that look like completeness are lies by layout.
  • No lucky passes on load-bearing outputs. Anything gating a large subtree must pass consistently across independent runs, not once.

Open questions for the next phase

  • The cold-start era. Mission one has no library, no templates, no benchmarks, no commons. The bootstrap period — where early missions double as institution-building — deserves its own design: seeded templates? deliberately easy first missions? imported starting playbooks?
  • Seeding the sealed bench. The constitutional benchmark vault needs verified missions before amendments can ever be evaluated — where do the first ones come from, and who curates them initially?
  • Human ratification bandwidth. The dial routes decisions to a human; at scale, that human becomes a bottleneck and a rubber-stamp risk of their own. What does the ratification workload actually look like, and what tooling does the human need?
  • Taxonomy governance. Capability categories drift as missions vary — when does the Learning Agent split, merge or retire categories, and what stops taxonomy churn from invalidating clade histories?
  • The v0 slice. Which subset of this architecture is the smallest thing that exercises the whole loop — decompose, contract, conjure, verify, fold, learn — end to end? (Recommendation: pick that slice by walking one real mission through this document on paper first.)

Verdict — ready for planning

Yes. The functional architecture is internally coherent, every mechanism traces to a decision made in the brainstorm or to documented evidence, and the open questions above are scoping questions, not holes in the concept. The recommended next step is a tabletop walkthrough — one concrete mission traced through every stage of this dossier by hand — followed by definition of the v0 slice, and only then the technical and non-functional design pass that was deliberately kept out of scope here.
