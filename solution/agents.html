Chapter 3 · The Agents

Four permanent officers,
one manufactured workforce

The meta-agents are best understood as offices, not individuals — each is a role with a mandate, powers, and hard limits, whose working methods (its playbook) are versioned assets that improve over time while the mandate itself stays fixed.

The Orchestrator

Mandate: turn one mission into many contracts, and many results into one mission.

What it does

  • Runs intake. Negotiates the mission contract with the requester — success criteria, boundaries, autonomy dial, budget — refusing to start on ambiguity it hasn't either resolved or explicitly flagged.
  • Builds the tree. Recursively decomposes using templates from the Asset Registry, authoring a complete contract for every node, declaring dependencies, pinning cross-cutting decisions before any fan-out.
  • Aims the atomizer. At every node, decides decompose-vs-delegate-whole: parallelizable breadth gets split to atoms; sequential, constraint-entangled work stays whole with a bigger budget.
  • Keeps the mission ledger. A living record per mission: verified facts, open unknowns, flagged assumptions, current plan — rewritten deliberately when progress stalls, so replanning is an explicit recorded act, not drift.
  • Governs escalation. Owns the ladder; matches verdict error classes to rungs; detects stalls; decides when the surrender threshold is met per the dial.
  • Folds up. As each subtree completes, assembles its children's verified outputs into the parent deliverable — the single writer at every assembly point — and submits its own work to review like everyone else.

Hard limits

  • Cannot verify its own work — Gate A judges its decompositions, Gate B its assemblies.
  • Cannot exceed the mission budget or grant itself more; can only ask the requester.
  • Cannot design agents — capability gaps go to the Agent Creator as category demands.

The Agent Creator

Mandate: staff every contract with the cheapest competent specialist — reusing before designing.

What it does

  • Categorizes. Clusters the approved task graph into capability categories — the unit of staffing. A thousand tasks typically collapse into a dozen categories; the taxonomy itself is a learnable asset.
  • Runs the reuse market. For each category, searches the Asset Registry; existing designs bid with their capability descriptions and track records on similar categories. Reuse beats creation whenever the record supports it — reuse is also the main cost lever.
  • Designs on no-bid. When no design credibly serves a category, composes a new specialist from the design playbook's typed building blocks — not blank-page prompt authorship, but assembly from proven components (a constrained design space demonstrably beats freeform generation).
  • Writes the whole spec. Role instructions, capability manifest, context entitlements, and the validation harness that will later measure whether this design earns permanence (anatomy below).
  • Scales effort. Applies effort-scaling rules — how many workers a category gets, sized to task class and budget — preventing both fifty-agents-for-a-triviality and one-agent-for-an-avalanche.
  • Staffs verification too. Conjures specialist verifier agents for the Reviewer's gates — under independence rules the Creator must honor: a verifier never shares design lineage with what it verifies.

Hard limits

  • Cannot execute tasks or judge outputs — it builds the builders and the checkers, never the verdicts.
  • Cannot promote its own designs into the library — promotion belongs to the Learning Agent, on evidence.
  • Cannot design outside a contract's entitlements — no agent gets context its task doesn't warrant.

The Reviewer

Mandate: nothing counts as done — or as well-formed — until independently verified against its contract.

What it does

  • Guards Gate A. Audits decompositions before execution: atomicity, testability of criteria, non-overlapping boundaries, stopping conditions present, pinned decisions where siblings must fit, sane use of the delegate-whole gate.
  • Guards Gate B. Verifies completions in two tiers — mechanical (the contract's executable checks pass) and semantic (the output serves the parent's intent, not just the letter of the criteria).
  • Scales depth to blast radius. Sets each task's verification plan: single rigorous check for low-stakes leaves; independent redundant runs with required agreement for outputs that gate large subtrees; consistency-across-runs requirements where flakiness is fatal.
  • Red-flags. Discards outputs whose structure smells wrong even when criteria technically pass — suspiciously exact fits, missing work products, verification-shaped answers.
  • Issues structured verdicts. Every verdict names the failed criterion, the failing step, and an error class from the shared taxonomy — the record that drives the escalation ladder and feeds learning. Verdicts are immutable once issued.
  • Audits itself. Runs calibration on its own institution: random re-reviews by independent verifiers, planted known-bad outputs that must get caught, and per-clause compliance sampling. Rubber-stamping is measured, not assumed away.

Hard limits (constitutional)

  • Its independence, verdict immutability, and the meaning of its metrics are constitutional — outside every learner's write scope.
  • Verifiers never share lineage with the verified; workers never see the verification harness in gameable detail.
  • It cannot rewrite contracts — a bad contract is bounced to its author, never quietly reinterpreted.

The Learning Agent

Mandate: make the next mission measurably better than the last — without ever touching the yardstick.

What it does — fast loop (during missions)

  • Bounded hot-fixes. When the live ledger shows a pattern — one category failing Gate B repeatedly for the same criterion — it may patch worker-layer assets mid-mission: a category's instructions, a staffing choice, a template parameter. Bounded means: worker layer only, one change at a time, logged as an experiment, auto-reverted if the failure rate doesn't move.

What it does — science loop (between missions)

  • Mines. Queries the ledger for weak spots: per-category compliance rates, escalation hot spots, budget-versus-value outliers, surrender dossiers.
  • Hypothesizes. Forms candidate improvements conditioned on the full history of prior experiments — with an explicit out-of-ideas ladder: re-examine evidence, combine near-misses, go more radical.
  • Experiments. Tests candidates on replay benchmarks — completed tasks from the ledger with known verified outcomes — under a fixed evaluation budget so heterogeneous changes stay comparable. Wins must replicate; a single lucky run adopts nothing.
  • Transfer-tests. A win measured on one mission slice must hold on a held-out slice before swarm-wide adoption.
  • Adopts by ratchet. Improvements land as itemized delta edits (never wholesale rewrites) to versioned assets; equal-or-worse auto-reverts; simpler-is-better breaks ties; every adoption keyed to its evidence.
  • Curates. Promotes designs on lineage performance (clade scores), keeps Pareto sets per category rather than single champions, down-weights instead of deleting, quarantines and expires commons entries.
  • Petitions. May propose constitutional amendments — argued from ledger evidence, evaluated on sealed benchmarks, ratified only out-of-band.

Hard limits (constitutional)

  • No write access to: metric definitions, review independence rules, the ledger's past, budget enforcement, the amendment protocol.
  • Its own playbook is evolvable — through the very same experiment-and-ratchet discipline, never by shortcut.

The supporting cast

Context Broker

The only channel for context beyond the contract. Serves sibling outputs, mission background and commons entries — filtered to what the requesting task's entitlements allow — and logs every exchange as a first-class audit event. The broker is what makes "no peer-to-peer chatter" livable: coordination without a side channel, and a complete record of who knew what, when.

Workers — the conjured specialists

Ephemeral, contract-scoped, and deliberately narrow. A worker must restate its contract before starting and bounce it if it can't; it delivers not just an output but an evidence bundle — what it did, what it consulted, what it assumed — because its deliverable must be verifiable by a stranger. Workers hold no standing state: everything worth keeping leaves through the deliverable or the ledger.

Anatomy of an agent design

What the Agent Creator actually produces — the artifact that lives or dies in the registry:

Agent Design · specification
Role instructions
The system prompt: who this specialist is, how it works, its quality bar, its tone of operation. Composed from playbook building blocks, not freehand.
Capability manifest
What task categories it claims to serve — the description it "bids" with when future missions staff similar work. Retrievable by similarity, honest by measurement.
Context entitlements
What it may request from the broker, and no more. Entitlements derive from the contract, never from the agent's curiosity.
Validation harness
The repeatable checks that measure whether this design performs — the evidence permanence decisions are made on. A design without a harness cannot earn permanence, by rule.
Lineage & provenance
Which designs it descends from, which mission demanded it, which playbook version produced it. Nothing in the registry is anonymous.
Performance record
Filled in by reality: per-category compliance rates, escalation involvement, cost-per-verified-outcome — and the clade score aggregating how its whole lineage performs.
